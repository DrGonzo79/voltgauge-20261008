# Office Hours: VoltGauge

Generated 2026-10-09 · startup diagnostic · pre-product

## Position

The broad “Carfax for EVs” framing is too wide and implies proprietary truth the product does not have. The paid wedge is **buyer-side triage before a pre-purchase inspection**: turn three listings into one evidence-backed inspection candidate.

## Six forcing questions

1. **Demand reality:** The source says used-EV buyers repeatedly ask whether the battery is dying. That is problem evidence, not payment evidence. No interviews, purchases, or conversion data were available.
2. **Status quo:** Buyers compare listing price/mileage, trust a seller’s generic battery claim, search owner forums, buy a battery report separately, then hunt for an EV-qualified mechanic. The cost is decision delay, unnecessary inspections, or a high-cost battery mistake.
3. **Desperate specificity:** First-time used-EV buyer, $15k–$30k budget, shopping outside warranty and unable to interpret state-of-health, charging, recall, or degradation signals. Consequence: a bad five-figure purchase.
4. **Narrowest paid wedge:** $49 shortlist review of up to three listings, with one recommended inspection target and a buyer brief. Concierge fulfillment comes before marketplace automation.
5. **Observation plan:** Watch 10 active buyers screen real listings without coaching. Record where they seek battery evidence, which caveats change a shortlist, and whether they pay $49 before inspection. A survey is insufficient.
6. **Future-fit:** As used EV volume and model diversity rise, battery provenance and charging compatibility become more important. The product becomes less valuable if manufacturer diagnostics become universal, standardized, and freely portable.

## Premises and alternatives

- **Minimal viable — concierge brief (recommended):** intake form + manual evidence gathering + standardized brief. Small effort, lowest trust risk, tests willingness to pay.
- **Prototype — deterministic self-serve triage:** shared fixtures and explainable scoring, as shipped here. Medium effort, useful for usability tests, but not market truth.
- **Ideal architecture — verified evidence network:** VIN ingestion, consented diagnostics, certified inspectors, comparable market data, and audit trails. XL effort and high integration risk.

## Success criteria and assignment

Within 14 days: 10 observed shopping sessions, 5 paid $49 briefs, and at least 3 buyers who use the brief to order an inspection. Assignment: recruit from two model-specific owner groups with a paid, limited-slot offer—not a waitlist.

## Open questions

Availability and legal use of OEM/telematics data; broker licensing by jurisdiction; liability for false negatives; inspector coverage and unit economics.
